# Editorial Review Checklist

This is the universal checklist applied to every article regardless of client. Each item has an ID for traceability in the report.

## Checklist Items

### CL-01: Positives
Identify and note genuine strengths in the writing — things the writer did well. This is important for writer morale and calibration. Look for strong hooks, clear explanations, good use of examples, effective structure, or compelling arguments.

### CL-02: Logical Flow
Information is in a logical order and there's a smooth flow from one thought to the next. Check that paragraphs connect naturally, transitions make sense, and the reader isn't left confused about how the article got from point A to point B.

### CL-03: Tone of Voice (TOV)
TOV aligns with client preferences. This requires cross-referencing the client content guidelines. Check for consistency throughout the article — sometimes writers start strong on TOV and drift mid-article.

### CL-04: Components and Client Requirements
Components, images, examples, and specific client requirements (e.g., TLDR sections, callout boxes, specific formatting) adhere to client guidelines. Check that all required structural elements are present and correctly formatted.

### CL-05: Links Quality
Internal links suit client guidelines and external links go to credible sources. Check that links are relevant to the surrounding text, not forced or spammy, and that external sources are authoritative (not random blogs or competitors where inappropriate).

### CL-06: Writing Quality
Writing quality is assessed: grammar, clarity, sentence variety, readability. Flag awkward phrasing, overly complex sentences, passive voice overuse, or weak word choices. Be specific about what should change and why.

### CL-07: Accuracy
Info is accurate: product features, pricing, functionality and processes; case studies and stats; definitions; statements and claims. This is critical — every factual statement that can be verified should be checked. Flag anything that seems wrong, outdated, or unsupported.

### CL-08: AI Content Detection
Flag any suspicious AI content — content must feel human-written. Look for: generic filler phrases, overly perfect structure, lack of personality, hedging language patterns typical of AI ("It's important to note that..."), unnaturally smooth transitions, or content that says a lot without saying anything specific.

### CL-09: Technical Preferences
Client technical preferences are addressed: metadata table contains Categories, Table of Contents, SEO box, and other client-specific items. Cross-reference the client guidelines for what must appear in the metadata section.

### CL-10: Meta Data
The titles and meta description adhere to client guidelines and SEO best practices. Check title length (typically under 60 characters), meta description length (typically 150-160 characters), keyword placement, and that they're compelling for click-through.

### CL-11: Clearscope
The Clearscope score is adequate and there are no low-hanging fruit terms in the unused terms list. If a Clearscope report is referenced or available, check the score and identify easy-to-add terms that would boost it.

### CL-12: Internal Links
Good density and no obvious missing links or critical missing links (e.g., missing a homepage or free trial link; missing feature page or product page links). Internal linking is essential for SEO — check that the article links to relevant product pages, related blog posts, and key conversion pages.

### CL-13: Intro Quality
Intro is concise, digs into pain/paints a paradise picture, tells the reader what they'll learn using second person language ("you'll learn"). The intro should hook the reader, make them feel understood, and promise value.

### CL-14: BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)
Friction is reduced by placing the most important info up front or answering the heading question. Readers scanning the article should be able to get the key takeaway from each section without reading every word.

### CL-15: Content Design
There's plenty of white space, images, headings, and other components (if needed). The article shouldn't be a wall of text. Check for appropriate use of subheadings, bullet points, images, tables, callout boxes, and other visual breaks.

### CL-16: Brief Alignment
The article follows the brief the writer was given. Check that the topic, target keywords, structure, angle, and any specific requirements from the brief have been addressed. Flag any significant deviations — missing sections the brief called for, a different angle than specified, or ignored instructions. Minor creative deviations are fine if they serve the article; major omissions or contradictions are not.

## Strategic Review Checklist

These items form a separate, lighter-touch review layer. They assess whether the article is strategically sound — not just editorially correct. Feedback here should be concise: a clear pass or fail with a brief explanation. This does NOT need the same depth of analysis as the Editorial Review items above, but passes and fails must still be accurate and grounded. Never invent strategic concerns that aren't supported by the article content and client context.

### SR-01: Product Connection
The product or brand connection is clear and natural, not forced. Readers understand how the product solves the pain introduced in the article.

### SR-02: Positioning Tone Match
The tone matches the product's positioning (expert, approachable, innovative, etc.). Cross-reference the client content guidelines for positioning cues.

### SR-03: Competitive Differentiation
The article differentiates from competitor content on the same topic. It brings a unique insight, framework, angle, or perspective rather than rehashing what's already ranking.

### SR-04: Audience Match
Tone, depth, and examples match the target reader (typically SMBs: business owners, sales leaders, marketers — but check client guidelines for specifics). The content assumes the right level of prior knowledge — neither overexplaining basics nor skipping necessary context.

### SR-05: Search Intent Alignment
Content meets the right search intent (informational, commercial, navigational). The framing connects to reader pain points and use cases relevant to the intent.

### SR-06: Time to Value
Gets to the intent quickly. The reader isn't wading through filler or excessive preamble before reaching the substance they came for.

### SR-07: Example Quality
Examples are purposeful and valuable — introduced and analysed in full, not just name-dropped. They are relevant to the target audience and the client's customer base. Each example should illuminate the point, not just fill space.
